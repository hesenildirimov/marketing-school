The hardest question in any marketing budget discussion is: "How much should we spend?" Data from the IPA Databank gives an empirical framework for answering it: ESOV.

## Two metrics

- **SOM (share of market)** — the brand's share of sales in its category.
- **SOV (share of voice)** — the brand's share of advertising spend in its category.

The difference between them is **ESOV = SOV − SOM** (excess share of voice).

## The observed pattern

According to the IPA data, brands with positive ESOV gain market share and brands with negative ESOV lose it. As a rough scale, every **10 points of positive ESOV** translates into about **0.5 points of market share growth** per year.

This is an average, not a guarantee. Creative quality, category dynamics and price positioning all change the outcome considerably. The direction, however, is consistent: a brand whose share of voice stays below its market share shrinks over the long term.

## A simple calculation

Say your brand has a market share of **12%**, total annual advertising spend in the category is **AZN 10 million**, and you spend **AZN 0.9 million**.

1. SOV = 0.9 / 10 = **9%**
2. SOM = **12%**
3. ESOV = 9 − 12 = **−3 points**

Conclusion: the current budget will not hold your share; you will gradually lose it. To hold share you need SOV ≈ SOM, or about AZN 1.2 million. To grow by 0.5 points a year you need ESOV ≈ +10, so SOV ≈ 22%, or about AZN 2.2 million.

> You need category spend data to calculate ESOV. Official ad-spend monitoring in Azerbaijan is limited, so building an estimate from media agency and tender data is standard practice.

## Limits of the framework

1. **If category spend is unknown,** the calculation becomes an estimate. Show the range of that estimate openly.
2. **Creative quality is a multiplier.** Strong creative can deliver results even with low ESOV; weak creative wastes high ESOV.
3. **A new brand starts with SOM close to zero,** so its ESOV looks formally large and positive — but the absolute spend may still fall short of what it takes to break into the market.
4. **The short-term/long-term split is a separate question.** Another finding from Binet and Field: splitting the budget roughly **60% brand building and 40% activation** gives the best average results in FMCG.

## How to use it in a presentation

ESOV is a strong argument when defending a budget to senior management, because it links spend to results and turns "we want more advertising" into a measurable hypothesis. Three numbers on one slide are enough: current SOV, current SOM and the SOV your target share requires.
